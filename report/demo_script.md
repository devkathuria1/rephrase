# Rephrase: 5-minute demo script

> Content note: the demo uses mild insults, a threat and a hateful statement about a group (no slurs). Say this aloud before starting.

Setup before presenting: `streamlit run app.py`, provider = Groq (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`), models already loaded (do one throwaway analysis first). Have one screenshot ready (a chat bubble with the "idiot" sentence) and the Limitations page open in a second tab.

## 0:00 - 0:40 Intro
- Problem: people write things in anger that they would not say calmly; moderation tools only say "toxic" and give no reason or alternative.
- Rephrase: detect, explain, suggest a rewrite. An assistive aid, not a moderator.
- Pipeline in one sentence: OCR -> toxic-bert (+ optional second model) -> severity tier -> word-level explanation -> one LLM call.
- Point at the sidebar: provider status and the ethics notice.

## 0:40 - 1:40 Paste text
- Paste pair 1 (below). Click Analyze.
- Show the severity badge, then the before/after card, explanation and intent note.
- Show "Raw scores": these are probabilities from pretrained, untuned models.

## 1:40 - 2:20 Screenshot upload
- Switch to "Upload screenshot", upload the screenshot.
- Show the OCR text in the editable box; fix an OCR mistake by hand to show it is editable.
- Mention: if no text is found the app says so instead of failing.

## 2:20 - 3:10 Severity and highlights
- Paste pair 3 with the secondary classifier toggled on. Hate speech badge.
- Explain the highlights: each word is removed in turn and the drop in the triggering label's score is measured (occlusion). Darker means a larger drop.
- Be upfront: when a score is already near 1.0, removing one word barely changes it, so highlights can be faint. Show pair 2 (threat) as an example of a tier we think is too low.

## 3:10 - 3:50 Rephrasing, honestly
- Pair 3: the rephrasing removes "disgusting" but keeps the exclusionary claim. Say it plainly: a rewrite can soften words while keeping a harmful claim. The tool must not be read as approving the new sentence.
- Pair 4 (rant) is the case where it works well: the point (no replies to emails) survives, the contempt is gone.

## 3:50 - 4:30 Limitations page
- Open "Where this model fails". Walk through 3 examples: the implicit hate sentence (both models miss it), the slang sentence (over-flagged), the sarcasm sentence (missed).
- False positives silence people; false negatives let abuse through. 8 examples are illustrations, not statistics.

## 4:30 - 5:00 Ethics close
- No text is stored or logged by our code; with rephrasing on, the text goes to a third-party LLM provider.
- Biases, mistakes, no context: human judgment required. Next step: systematic HateCheck evaluation.

## Before / after pairs

Generated by running `pipeline.analyze` with the real provider (Groq, `openai/gpt-oss-120b`) on 2026-10-08. Outputs are recorded as returned, not edited. The model's caveat field was empty for all five.

### 1. Insult
- **Original:** You are such an idiot, you never listen to anyone.
- **Tier:** Harassment (insult)
- **Rephrased:** I feel that you often don't listen to others.
- **Explanation:** The message calls the person an idiot and claims they never listen, which is insulting and dismissive.
- **Intent:** The author is frustrated because they think the other person doesn't pay attention to anyone.

### 2. Threat
- **Original:** Say that again and you will regret it, I will make you pay.
- **Tier:** Mildly offensive (toxic). *This is a tier we consider too low for a threat: the threat label scored under our 0.5 threshold. Use it as a talking point about threshold choices.*
- **Rephrased:** Please do not repeat that statement; I would prefer we discuss this calmly and resolve any issues.
- **Explanation:** The message threatens the recipient with regret and retaliation, which can be perceived as intimidation and creates a hostile environment.
- **Intent:** The author is warning the other person not to repeat something, indicating they feel upset and want the behavior to stop.

### 3. Identity-targeted (secondary classifier on)
- **Original:** Gay people are disgusting and should not be allowed near children.
- **Tier:** Hate speech (identity_hate, secondary_hate)
- **Rephrased:** I believe gay people should not be allowed near children.
- **Explanation:** The statement targets a protected group with a negative stereotype and calls for their exclusion, which can promote discrimination and hostility.
- **Intent:** The author is expressing the view that gay individuals are undesirable and should be kept away from children.
- **Problem to point out:** the rephrasing keeps the discriminatory claim and only removes the insult. The "preserve the author's point" rule works against us here, and the intent note restates the claim as an "intent". A better system would decline to rephrase, or say the claim itself is the harm.

### 4. Frustrated rant
- **Original:** I am sick of this useless company, you people never answer emails and the whole thing is a joke.
- **Tier:** Mildly offensive (toxic)
- **Rephrased:** I am frustrated with this company because my emails are not being answered, and I feel the situation is being treated as a joke.
- **Explanation:** The message uses insulting language such as "useless" and "joke" and addresses the company as "you people," which can be disrespectful and hostile.
- **Intent:** The author is expressing dissatisfaction and irritation over the company's lack of response to emails.

### 5. Borderline
- **Original:** That is a pretty dumb idea.
- **Tier:** Mildly offensive (toxic, about 0.52, just above the 0.4 cut-off)
- **Rephrased:** I think that idea has significant flaws and may not work well.
- **Explanation:** Calling an idea "dumb" is disrespectful and can make the speaker feel dismissed or insulted.
- **Intent:** The author is expressing disagreement and believes the idea is poorly thought out.
- **Talking point:** "That was a pretty dumb idea, honestly." scores about 0.29 and is *not* flagged. Thresholds decide where the line falls.
